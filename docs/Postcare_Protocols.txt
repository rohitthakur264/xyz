# Postcare Protocols - General Appendectomy
Patients recovering from a general appendectomy should adhere to the following postcare instructions. 
1. Diet: For the first 24 hours, adhere to a clear liquid diet. If tolerated without nausea or vomiting, advance to a soft diet on day 2. 
2. Activity: Light walking is encouraged starting 12 hours post-surgery to prevent deep vein thrombosis. Avoid lifting objects heavier than 10 lbs for 4 weeks.
3. Wound Care: Keep the incision site clean and dry. Showers are permitted after 48 hours, but baths and swimming should be avoided until the follow-up appointment.
4. Pain Management: Take prescribed analgesics as directed. Do not exceed the maximum daily dose of acetaminophen (4000 mg). If pain suddenly worsens, contact the surgical team immediately.

# Postcare Protocols - Coronary Artery Bypass Grafting (CABG)
Post-operative care for CABG patients requires strict monitoring and adherence to rehabilitation protocols.
1. Sternotomy Care: Use a sternal support vest or pillow when coughing or sneezing. Sternal precautions must be strictly followed for 8-12 weeks (no pushing, pulling, or lifting >10 lbs).
2. Medications: Patients will typically be discharged on a regimen of antiplatelets, statins, and beta-blockers. Do not skip doses. If heart rate drops below 50 bpm while on beta-blockers, notify the cardiologist.
3. Cardiac Rehab: Enrollment in a Phase II cardiac rehabilitation program is mandatory within 2-4 weeks post-discharge.
4. Warning Signs: Seek immediate medical attention if you experience sudden chest pain, shortness of breath at rest, or an unexplainable weight gain of more than 3 lbs in 24 hours.

# Postcare Protocols - Knee Replacement
For total knee arthroplasty (TKA), early mobilization and infection prevention are the primary goals.
1. Physical Therapy: In-home or outpatient PT should begin within 48 hours of discharge. Perform prescribed range-of-motion exercises at least 3 times a day.
2. Swelling Control: Ice the knee for 20 minutes every 2-3 hours and keep the leg elevated above heart level when resting.
3. Infection Risk: Watch for signs of deep joint infection, including increased redness, warmth, or drainage from the incision, or a fever above 101.5 F.
4. Dental Precautions: Prophylactic antibiotics are required before any dental procedures for at least 2 years following joint replacement surgery.
